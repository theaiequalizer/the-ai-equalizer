Start here

Build your own tutor in ten minutes. Free. No signup with us, ever.

You watched the video. Now do the thing. You need your phone or a laptop, a pen, paper, and ten minutes. That is the whole initiation fee. Some students used to pay a lot more than that for a lot less than this.

1. Open an AI app (2 min)

Pick one. The free account is enough to start: Claude (claude.ai), ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), or Gemini (gemini.google.com). If you already have one on your phone, use that one. Done is better than perfect.

2. Make it a home, not a chat (1 min)

In Claude, create a Project. In ChatGPT, a Project or custom GPT. In Gemini, a Gem. Name it My DiffEq Tutor. A regular chat forgets you. The whole point of this tutor is that by week eight it knows your mistakes better than you do.

3. Copy the template: the tutor's rules (1 min)

Press the button, then paste everything into your project's instructions box.

Copied. Paste it into your project's instructions.

4. Add the course pack: the tutor's playbook (1 min)

The template is the personality. This is the playbook: the map of the course, how every problem type is attacked, the classic traps, and the exam craft. Copy it, paste it into a note or document, save that as a file, and upload the file into your project's knowledge or files section. (In Claude you can also paste it straight in as text content.)

Copied. Save as a file and upload it to your project.

Prefer the file itself? Get it here.

5. Fill in five blanks (2 min)

At the top of the template you pasted there are bracketed lines: your name, school, course and professor, textbook, exam dates. Fill them in. Skip what you do not know yet. This is the moment it stops being our tutor and becomes yours.

6. Give it your course (2 min)

Upload your syllabus into the project. A photo of it works. Problem sets and returned quizzes too; returned exams are gold, they are your professor's actual voice. Keep a folder on your own device with the same materials, one subfolder per unit; your archive, under your control, forever. No syllabus handy? Start anyway and add it tomorrow.

7. First session (right now, 5 min)

Grab the pen. Work any problem from your current homework, on paper, as far as you can get. Stuck is fine. Wrong is fine. Photograph the page, send the photo, and type one word: check

It will find where your work goes wrong, name the kind of mistake, and ask you a question so you can fix it yourself. It will not give you the answer. It is not allowed to. You are training for a room it cannot enter with you.

The seven words you now own

check + photo: find my error, do not fix it
stuck + photo: one hint, just one
why: explain the idea under this step, plain words first
drill: give me another one like it
harder: I got it, raise the bar
exam me: a timed quiz from my own weak spots
log: end the session, write down what tripped me

End every session with log. That log becomes a record of your own mind, and by midterm week it will be the most valuable document you own.
